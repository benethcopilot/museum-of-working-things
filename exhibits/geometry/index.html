Compass and Straightedge Stars

The star patterns that cover mosques, palaces and madrasas from Spain to Uzbekistan are built from circles and squares, and need nothing but a compass and a straightedge to draw. No measuring, no numbers: only circles swung from points you already have, and lines laid between them. Construct a star step by step, then cover a wall with it.

Step 1

    Repeat the tile

    Each finished tile repeats edge to edge, so the stars meet their neighbours point to point and the pattern could go on forever. Where a star falls in a corner of the panel, exactly a quarter of it shows; along an edge, exactly half.

    Circles, squares and stars

    Many designs start from a circle, which stood for unity. A 15th-century mosque in Yazd, Persia, is decorated from one circle divided into six by six more circles around it, each passing through the centre: the same flower you drew. On that grid sits a six-pointed star. The eight-pointed star, everywhere in Islamic tilework, is simply two squares, one turned 45° against the other.

    The forms reach into every craft: Persian girih strapwork, Moroccan zellij mosaics of glazed tiles set in plaster, jali pierced-stone screens, carpets, leather bindings, woodwork and stained glass. The Topkapı Scroll, drawn in Timurid Iran around 1500, collects 114 patterns as working drawings for builders.

Lock and Dam No. 1

Between Minneapolis and St. Paul the Mississippi drops 35.9 feet at the Ford Dam. Boats climb that step in a concrete box with gates at each end. You run the lock. There are no pumps here, only valves, gates and gravity.

    Gravity does the lifting

    Opening the filling valve lets the river above run down through a tunnel called a culvert and up through ports in the chamber floor. The water stops rising when it matches the river above, because water finds its own level.

    To lower a boat, the emptying valve lets the chamber drain to the river below. A full chamber holds about 6 million gallons above the river below. That much water would run the dam’s four turbines for about two minutes.

    Why the gates point upstream

    Seen from above, each pair of gates meets in a shallow V that points upstream. The higher water pushes the two leaves against each other, so the harder the river pushes, the tighter they seal. Miter gates are credited to Leonardo da Vinci in the late 1400s.

    Why you must level first

    Water pushes harder the deeper it is. With the full lift on one side, the push on a pair of these 56-foot gates is at least 1,100 tons. No gate machinery can swing that, and the lock’s controls won’t try.

    The lower gates are the tall ones. They hold back the whole lift plus the water below it. The upper gates only reach down to the upper sill, a concrete step at the head of the chamber that you can see when the chamber is empty.

    A dam built twice as high

    1. 1907

      The first lock and dam finished on the Mississippi opens near Meeker Island, upstream of today’s Lock 1. On May 19 the Itura is the first boat through. It was then called Lock and Dam No. 2.

    2. 1910

      Congress changes the plan. Instead of two low dams, Lock and Dam No. 1 will be a high dam that can make water power, and the lock is raised to a lift of 35.9 feet. Its pool will drown the Meeker Island dam.

    3. 1912

      Engineers cut five feet off the top of the Meeker Island dam so boats can pass over it. Part of its old lock wall still stands in the river.

    4. 1917

      On July 3 the lock opens with the lighthouse tender Dandelion carrying dignitaries from both cities. Its upper gates are the old lower gates from Meeker Island, floated downstream and reused.

    5. 1924

      Henry Ford finishes a powerhouse on the east end of the dam to run his new Twin Cities car assembly plant on the bluff above. People start calling it the Ford Dam.

    6. 1932

      After part of the lock failed in 1929 and stopped river traffic, a second chamber is finished beside the first. Lock 1 is still the only twin lock in the Corps of Engineers’ St. Paul District.

    7. 1983

      A five-year rebuilding ends, and computer controls replace much of the old manual and hydraulic machinery.

    8. 2015

      The Upper St. Anthony Falls lock in Minneapolis closes for good to slow invasive carp moving up the river. Lock 1 still opens each season for barges and pleasure boats.

    • 35.9 ft lift, second highest on the Mississippi until 1963

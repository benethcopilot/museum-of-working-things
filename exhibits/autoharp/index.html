Learn chords on an

Autoharp

An autoharp has a string for nearly every note and a row of wooden bars lined with felt. Press a bar and its felt silences every string that doesn’t belong to the chord. Strum, and only the chord rings. That is what a chord is: the notes that are left.

Drag up or down across the strings to strum, or press Space. Press a button below to hold its bar down.

The fifteen bars of the standard factory layout, in its two rows.

The bar you are holding

None

    How chords work

    Five short lessons. Each button plays its example on the harp above.

    Every note at once

    With no bar pressed, all 36 strings ring together. Every note sounds and nothing fits. That cloud is why the bars exist.

    Major: a big third, then a small one

    Start on a note, the root. Go up four semitones to the major third, then three more to the fifth. C, E and G make C major: bright and settled.

    Minor: the small third first

    Swap the order. Three semitones, then four. D, F and A make D minor. Only one note moves, from F♯ down to F, and the mood turns darker.

    Sevenths want to go home

    Add one more third on top. G7 is G, B, D and F. The B leans up toward C and the F leans down toward E, so G7 pulls toward a C chord.

    Three chords carry a song

    In the key of C, C is chord I, F is IV and G is V. Most folk songs need only those three. On this harp they sit side by side.

    Play along

    Listen first, then take a turn. On your turn you press the bars and the harp keeps strumming.

    Who made the autoharp

    In December 1881 Charles F. Zimmermann, a German-born instrument maker in Philadelphia, applied for a patent on a zither with dampers that silenced chosen strings, and named it the autoharp. The patent was issued in May 1882. The design behind today’s instrument came from Karl August Gütter of Markneukirchen, Germany, whose Volkszither he patented in Britain around 1883–84. From 1885 Zimmermann built autoharps on Gütter’s pattern.

    This harp

    The common modern autoharp has 36 strings, from F2 up to C6. It is not fully chromatic: covering that range note for note would take 44 strings, so a few low notes, and the G♯ below middle C, are left out. This one has the fifteen bars of the standard factory layout. Each key’s main chords sit close together on it: B♭, C7 and F; F, G7 and C; C, D7 and G.

Machines & Messages · Lyon, 1804

Jacquard Loom

A silk weaver once needed a helper to pull the right threads for every row of a pattern. Joseph Marie Jacquard replaced the helper with a chain of punched cards. Draw a design, turn it into cards, and weave it.

The design

Squared point paper, one square per thread crossing. Coloured squares show the warp on top.

Cards

32 cards

The loom

Each card lifts the warp threads that line up with its holes. The shuttle carries the weft under them and over the rest.

Weave

Silk

Designs to start from

Holes lift threads

For each throw of the shuttle, the loom presses one card against a row of rods. Where there is a hole, a rod passes through and its hook stays in place, and a rising bar lifts that hook’s warp thread. Where there is no hole, the rod is pushed aside and its thread stays down.

Weaving by numbers

Every hole is a yes or a no. A pattern became a stack of decisions that a machine could follow without understanding it, and that anyone could swap for another stack. This 32 by 32 design needs 32 cards. Real Jacquard looms read hundreds of holes from every card.

From silk to computers

“The Analytical Engine weaves algebraical patterns just as the Jacquard-loom weaves flowers and leaves.”

Ada Lovelace, writing in 1843 about Charles Babbage’s design for a calculating engine that would read its instructions from punched cards.

A chain of cards to the computer

  1. 1725

    Basile Bouchon, a Lyon weaver’s son, controls a loom with a roll of punched paper. Jean-Baptiste Falcon soon replaces the roll with separate pasteboard cards.

  2. 1804

    Joseph Marie Jacquard builds a loom that reads a chain of cards automatically, one per throw of the shuttle, so a single weaver can make patterned silk.

  3. 1805

    Napoleon and Josephine visit Lyon in April to see the new loom.

  4. 1839

    A portrait of Jacquard is woven in silk on his loom. It takes 24,000 punched cards. Charles Babbage owns a copy.

  5. 1843

    Ada Lovelace compares Babbage’s Analytical Engine, which would read instructions from punched cards, to the Jacquard loom.

  6. 1886

    Lyon weavers produce the Livre de Prières, a prayer book woven entirely in silk, page by page, from punched cards.

  7. 1890

    Herman Hollerith counts the United States census with punched cards and tabulating machines. Punched cards carry computer data until the 1980s.

Warp and weft

The warp is the set of long threads stretched on the loom. The weft is the thread the shuttle carries across. Every square on the point paper is one place where they cross, and the only question for that square is which one lies on top.

Plain weave alternates every square. Twill steps each row over by one, which makes the diagonal lines you can see in denim.

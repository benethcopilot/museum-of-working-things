Foucault’s Pendulum

Léon Foucault hung a 28-kilogram ball from the dome of the Panthéon on a 67-metre wire and set it swinging. Over the hours, its swing seemed to turn slowly around the floor. The pendulum wasn’t turning. The building, and the whole Earth, was turning underneath it.

Why it depends on where you are

At the North Pole the floor turns right round underneath the pendulum once a day, in 23 hours 56 minutes. At the equator the floor isn’t turning around the straight-up direction at all, so the swing never changes. In between, the floor turns by the Earth’s spin times the sine of the latitude.

North of the equator the swing turns clockwise seen from above; south of it, anticlockwise.

Proof you could watch

  1. Feb 1851

    Foucault shows his pendulum to the public for the first time, in the meridian room of the Paris Observatory.

  2. Mar 1851

    A few weeks later he hangs the famous one in the Panthéon: a brass-coated lead bob of 28 kg on a 67 m wire. Each swing takes about 16.5 seconds, and the edge of the swing creeps about 5 mm further round every time.

  3. 1851

    He explains it in a paper to the French Academy of Sciences: the pendulum keeps its plane in space while the Earth turns beneath it.

  4. 1852

    He shows the same thing with a spinning gyroscope, a word he coined.

  5. 1855

    The original bob moves to the Conservatoire des Arts et Métiers in Paris.

  6. 1995

    An exact copy goes back under the Panthéon’s dome, where it still swings.

What the pendulum shows

People had accepted for two centuries that the Earth spins, from astronomy and arithmetic. Foucault’s pendulum was the first simple experiment that let anyone stand in a room and watch the rotation happen.

In Paris the swing turns about 11.3 degrees an hour and takes about 31.8 hours to come full circle. Museums put a ring of pegs around the floor so visitors can see the turning as the bob knocks them over one by one.

The bob here swings much faster than a real one, and the turning is sped up too, so you can watch a day go by in minutes.

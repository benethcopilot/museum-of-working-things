Planispheric astrolabe

The sky over Greenwich

Brass, engraved in your browser. Plate for latitude 51° 29′ N.

An astrolabe is a model of the sky you can hold. The plate underneath is engraved for one latitude: the horizon, circles of altitude rising to the point overhead, and lines of direction. The pierced rete on top is the sky. Its flames point to bright stars, and its offset ring is the ecliptic, the sun’s yearly road through the zodiac. Turn the rete as the sky turns and the instrument tells the hour, the sunrise and which stars are up.

    Sky puzzles

      Drag the instrument round to turn the rete through the day. On a phone, swipe sideways. With the instrument focused, the arrow keys turn it a quarter hour and Home returns to now.

      Horizon
      The long arc across the plate. Everything inside it is above the ground. The small circles nesting toward the top mark altitude, up to the zenith.
      Rete
      Each flame’s tip is a star. The sun sits on the ecliptic ring at today’s place in the zodiac and moves round it once a year.
      Rule
      The straight bar lies across the sun. Where it meets the rim, the Roman numerals give sundial time, with noon at the top.
      Night hours
      The curves below the horizon split the night into twelve unequal hours, the way time was kept before clocks.

The carousel

Philadelphia Toboggan Company carousel No. 33 was built in 1914 for the Minnesota State Fair: 68 horses and two chariots, carved from basswood. It ran only a few days a year for most of a century. In 1988 the fair let its lease lapse, and the carousel was nearly sold off piece by piece until a nonprofit and a gift from Gerard Cafesjian bought it whole. It has turned in Como Park since 2000.

The organ

Its band organ is a Wurlitzer Style 153, restored by Mike Merrick over about six years for the carousel’s reopening. It reads Style 150 rolls, seven inches wide with 54 positions. The Duplex frame holds two rolls, so one can rewind while the other plays. Inside are violin, flute and wooden trumpet pipes for the melody, stopped flutes and violins for the accompaniment, wooden trombones and diapason pipes in the bass, bell bars, and a bass drum, snare and cymbal. Holes at the edge of the roll switch the bells on and off and open the swell shutters.

The roll playing on the day this was made, arranged by Rich Olsen, included Dance by the Light of the Moon, built on the 1844 tune Buffalo Gals, which is on Roll 1 here. Its other songs are still under copyright.
